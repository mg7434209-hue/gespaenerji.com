Why is it needed?

## Drowning isn't loud like in the movies. It's silent.

A real drowning usually happens within 20-60 seconds, without splashing or shouting. In a crowded hotel pool, that moment is short and silent enough to escape even the most attentive lifeguard.

Worldwide, drowning is among the leading causes of death for children aged 1-4.

Lifeguard supervision is indispensable; but the human eye has physiological limits: glare, reflections, crowds and fatigue.

Technology exists to close those gaps: a tireless extra pair of eyes watching the entire pool.

### Supervision gap

In almost every case the child entered the water in the minutes a parent stepped away or lost sight of them in the crowd. In most incidents nearby guests did not notice what was happening.

### Insufficient lifeguard cover

Regulations set a mandatory number of lifeguards based on pool area and slide entry/exit points. Understaffing, gaps at shift changes and lunch breaks, and unmonitored blind spots are the recurring allegations.

### Age–pool mismatch

The four-year-old who died in 2026 was in the adult pool. The lack of physical separation, barriers and access control between children's and adult pools is a recurring risk factor.

### Management liability

In legal proceedings liability does not stop at the lifeguard: general managers, security managers and occupational safety specialists are also arrested or found at fault. Charges of causing death by negligence have become standard.

**🛟 Which of these gaps does the system close?**

The AI layer targets the supervision gap and blind spots: the pool surface is scanned continuously during shift changes, lunch breaks and peak hours, and a located alert reaches the lifeguard's watch the moment a risk appears. It does not replace the lifeguard, nor does it reduce the mandatory headcount — it closes the physiological limit of the human eye and turns every alert into an auditable record.

Business risk

## A single incident can cost far more than a season's earnings

A single fatal incident in the pool is not just a one-day crisis for a hotel:

- **Criminal proceedings:** In Turkish cases liability is not limited to the lifeguard; general managers, security managers and occupational safety specialists may also face negligent-homicide proceedings.
- **Compensation claims:** Material and moral damages litigation can drag on for years and occupy management's agenda.
- **Administrative sanctions:** Pool or facility operations may be restricted during the investigation.
- **A permanent reputation record:** News of the incident stays visible in search results alongside the facility's name for years, influencing agency and guest decisions long after.
- **Team attrition:** The trauma experienced by the lifeguard and operations team can turn into mid-season staff loss.

No technology can guarantee detecting every incident. But an additional surveillance layer that watches continuously and records every alert helps manage both the risk itself and the answer to "were reasonable precautions taken?". The monthly service fee is small next to the items above.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the system replace the lifeguard?

No — and it shouldn't. The international ISO 20380 standard defines these systems as a secondary supervision layer supporting the lifeguard. It extends your lifeguard's field of view and reaction speed.

### Can we reduce our lifeguard count once the system is installed?

No. Lifeguard numbers and qualifications are set by regulation; the system does not reduce or remove that obligation. ISO 20380 likewise defines this technology as a secondary surveillance layer supporting the lifeguard. Its contribution is not staff savings: it extends your existing team's field of view to the whole pool, delivers location information within seconds at the moment of risk, and turns every alert into a record you can show in audits.

### Can it be installed in our existing pool? How long does installation take?

Yes. In most projects installation is possible without draining the pool or closing the business; cabling and mounting can be scheduled for hours when the pool is closed. Total duration depends on the number of pools and existing infrastructure; the exact schedule is committed in the written quote after the free survey. After installation the system is calibrated for your facility and your lifeguard team receives on-site training. Facilities that prefer not to start mid-season can reserve next season's installation slot now.

### Does it work with our existing CCTV cameras?

Standard security cameras are designed for general surveillance; reflections, glare and viewing-angle issues on the water surface usually make them insufficient for drowning detection. The system runs on its own cameras designed for this task. That said, your existing cabling, network and recording infrastructure is assessed during the free survey; suitable parts can be reused, which can reduce installation time and cost.

### How many cameras are needed?

The camera count is engineered from the pool's surface area, shape, depth zones and blind spots; the goal is continuous coverage of the entire surface. A single regular-shaped pool may need only a few cameras, while pools with slides or divided sections need a larger coverage plan. The exact count and layout are presented in the written quote with a coverage sketch prepared at the free survey.

### Are there false alarms?

No system can promise zero false alarms; quality systems are designed to keep them to a few per day and are calibrated per facility. Every alert is stored as a record and report.

### How is guest privacy protected?

Footage is processed on an in-facility server and is not transferred to the cloud or abroad. The recording policy is configured to keep only incident moments, in line with data-protection law, and privacy notices are displayed at facility entrances.

### Do we have any obligations for KVKK (data protection) compliance?

With installation you receive pool-specific privacy notice and retention policy templates; your facility's main step is displaying these visibly at entrances and adding them to your existing KVKK documentation. Cameras are engineered to see only the pool and its surroundings; changing cabins, showers and similar private areas are excluded from coverage. Footage is processed on the on-site local server and is not transferred to the cloud or abroad. For the final legal assessment we recommend having your own KVKK counsel approve the texts.
